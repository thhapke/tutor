# French Grammar Framework (A1 to C1)

# A1
## 1. Subject Pronouns and Core Auxiliary Verbs
Mastering *je*, *tu*, *il/elle/on*, *nous*, *vous*, *ils/elles* alongside the absolute foundational conjugations of *être* (to be) and *avoir* (to have) in the present indicative tense. This includes understanding the dual nature of *on* (informal 'we' or impersonal 'one') and the vital distinction between informal *tu* and formal/plural *vous*.

## 2. Regular Verb Conjugation in the Present Tense
Conjugating regular verbs ending in *-er* (first conjugation group like *parler*), *-ir* (second group like *finir*), and common irregular *-re* verbs in the present indicative (*présent de l'indicatif*). This forms the baseline for constructing simple, affirmative, and negative statements using the standard *ne... pas* framework.

## 3. Definite, Indefinite, and Partitive Articles
Distinguishing between definite articles (*le, la, l', les*), indefinite articles (*un, une, des*), and partitive articles (*du, de la, de l'*). Learners must understand how articles change according to gender and number, and how partitive articles shift entirely to *de* or *d'* in negative sentences to express a total absence of quantity.

## 4. Noun and Adjective Agreement
Applying grammatical gender (masculine/feminine) and number (singular/plural) rules systematically across nouns and descriptive adjectives. This topic covers standard morphological changes (adding *-e* for feminine, *-s* for plural) as well as elementary irregular adjustments (e.g., *-eux* to *-euse*, *-al* to *-aux*).

## 5. Basic Possessive and Demonstrative Adjectives
Indicating ownership using possessive adjectives (*mon, ton, son, notre, votre, leur*, etc.) and pointing out specific objects using demonstrative adjectives (*ce, cet, cette, ces*). A critical sub-rule here is using masculine possessives before feminine singular nouns that begin with a vowel or a silent 'h' (e.g., *mon amie*).

## 6. Asking Questions
Structuring basic questions using three distinct methods: intonation (raising the voice at the end of a statement), the prefix *est-ce que*, and simple inversion of the subject and verb. It also includes using interrogative words like *qui*, *que*, *où*, *quand*, *pourquoi*, and *comment*.

# A2
## 7. Le Passé Composé with Avoir and Être
Expressing completed actions in the past using the *passé composé*. This involves mastering which verbs take *avoir* and which take *être* (the "house of être" verbs and all pronominal verbs), alongside the mandatory agreement rules for past participles conjugated with *être*.

## 8. L'Imparfait (The Imperfect Tense)
Forming and applying the *imparfait* to describe ongoing past states, background settings, habitual actions, or descriptions in the past. Learners discover how to derive the stem from the present-tense *nous* form and apply uniform endings (*-ais, -ais, -ait, -ions, -iez, -aient*).

## 9. Passé Composé vs. Imparfait Aspectual Interplay
Navigating the critical distinction between the *passé composé* (specific, completed events that advance a timeline) and the *imparfait* (continuous, background actions). This topic focuses heavily on sentences where an ongoing action in the *imparfait* is suddenly interrupted by a specific event in the *passé composé*.

## 10. Reflexive and Pronominal Verbs
Conjugating verbs that require a reflexive pronoun (*se laver*, *se souvenir*) in present and past tenses. This topic introduces the complexity of past participle agreement within the *passé composé*, where the participle agrees with the reflexive pronoun only if it acts as a direct object.

## 11. Direct and Indirect Object Pronouns (COD and COI)
Replacing nouns with direct object pronouns (*me, te, le, la, nous, vous, les*) and indirect object pronouns (*me, te, lui, nous, vous, leur*). This section covers single-pronoun placement directly before the conjugated verb, or before the infinitive if a dual-verb structure is present.

## 12. The Adverbial Pronouns 'Y' and 'En'
Mastering the replacement of spatial and prepositional phrases using *y* (replacing phrases introduced by *à*, meaning "there") and *en* (replacing phrases introduced by *de*, meaning "some", "any", or "of it"). It covers their mandatory placement before the verb and their usage in basic commands.

## 13. Basic Future Tenses (Futur Proche and Futur Simple)
Contrasting the *futur proche* (formed with *aller* + infinitive for immediate or highly certain events) with the *futur simple* (formed by adding specific endings to the future stem for distant or formal predictions). This marks the learner's first encounter with systematic future irregular stems.

# B1
## 14. Introduction to the Present Subjunctive Mood
Forming and applying the *subjonctif présent* to express subjectivity, necessity, emotions, desires, or doubt following the conjunction *que*. This shifts the student from objective statements of fact (indicative) to expressions of mental stance (e.g., *Il faut que tu saches...*).

## 15. Real and Possible Conditionals (Si Clauses Type 1 & 2)
Structuring conditional hypotheses using *si*. Type 1 models real, probable events (*si* + present -> present/future). Type 2 models imaginary or hypothetical present situations (*si* + imparfait -> conditionnel présent), requiring a clean mastery of the present conditional mood endings.

## 16. Relative Pronouns (Qui, Que, Où, Dont)
Connecting clauses seamlessly using basic and intermediate relative pronouns. Learners master *qui* as a subject replacement, *que* as a direct object replacement, *où* for times and places, and *dont* as a replacement for any object or phrase governed by the preposition *de*.

## 17. Multi-Pronoun Placement and Ordering
Arranging multiple pronouns correctly within a single clause. This topic establishes the rigid hierarchical grid of pronoun placement (*me/te/se* before *le/la/les* before *lui/leur* before *y* before *en*) across declarative statements, negative sentences, and compound tenses.

## 18. Comparative and Superlative Formations
Forming relative evaluations of nouns, adjectives, and adverbs using *plus... que*, *moins... que*, and *aussi/autant... que*. This includes mastering systematic irregular modifications such as *bon* becoming *meilleur* (adjective) and *bien* transforming into *mieux* (adverb) in comparative states.

## 19. The Plus-que-parfait (Past Perfect)
Constructing actions that took place prior to another past action using the *plus-que-parfait*. It is formed using the *imparfait* of the auxiliary verb (*avoir* or *être*) combined with the past participle, adding structural depth to complex retrospective storytelling.

# B2
## 20. Advanced Subjunctive Triggers and Conjunctions
Expanding the use of the subjunctive mood into complex adverbial clauses dictated by specific conjunctions (e.g., *bien que*, *pourvu que*, *avant que*, *jusqu'à ce que*). It also covers situations where the subjunctive is triggered by superlative statements or negative/interrogative expressions of opinion.

## 21. Unreal Past Conditionals (Si Clauses Type 3)
Formulating unfulfilled historical hypotheses and structural regrets about the past using Type 3 *si* clauses. This requires combining *si* + *plus-que-parfait* with the *conditionnel passé* (past conditional), testing the student's mastery of multi-layered auxiliary configurations.

## 22. Passive Voice Construction and Alternatives
Transforming active sentences into the passive voice (*la voix passive*) using *être* + past participle followed by the agent agent introduction (*par* or *de*). This module also emphasizes semantic passive alternatives used frequently by native speakers, such as using *on* or pronominal reflections.

## 23. Double Pronoun Order in the Imperative Mood
Navigating the shifting rules of pronoun order when using affirmative commands (*l'impératif affirmatif*), where pronouns move *after* the verb, connect via hyphens, change forms (*me/te* become *moi/toi*), and adopt a completely reversed relative order (e.g., *Donne-le-moi !*).

## 24. Complex Relative Pronouns (Lequel and Compound Forms)
Utilizing variable relative pronouns that must agree in gender and number with their antecedents when preceded by prepositions. This covers *lequel, laquelle, lesquels, lesquelles*, as well as their contracted forms with *à* (*auquel*) and *de* (*duquel*), commonly found in formal contexts.

## 25. The Gerund and Present Participle
Forming and distinguishing between the present participle (*participe présent*) acting as an adjective or clause-linker, and the gerund (*gérondif* introduced by *en*) used to express simultaneity ("while doing"), causality ("by doing"), or conditionality.

# C1
## 26. Le Passé Simple and Passé Antérieur
Mastering the recognition and literary usage of the *passé simple* and its compound counterpart, the *passé antérieur*. While obsolete in spoken language, full structural knowledge of these historical tenses is required at C1 to comprehend and write formal academic, historical, and literary texts.

## 27. The Subjunctive Past and Imperfect Moods
Employing the *subjonctif passé* for past subjective events, and recognizing the highly formal *subjonctif imparfait* / *plus-que-parfait* in classical or high-tier literary prose. C1 speakers must understand how these structures operate within classical rules governing the sequence of tenses (*la concordance des temps*).

## 28. Inversion After Initial Adverbs and Conjunctions
Applying formal stylistic inversion of the subject and verb following specific starting adverbs or restrictive expressions (such as *à peine*, *peut-être*, *en vain*, or *du moins*). This device is crucial for achieving an authentic, sophisticated cadence in advanced argumentative essays.

## 29. The Pleonastic 'Ne' (Le Ne Explétif)
Identifying and using the stylistically advanced, non-negative *ne explétif* in formal registers. This element appears without *pas* after specific verbs of fear (*craindre que*), preventatives (*éviter que*), expressions of doubt, or after the comparative conjunction *plus que* (e.g., *Il est plus intelligent qu'il ne le paraît*).

## 30. Indefinite Relative Pronouns (Quiconque, Quoi que, Où que)
Constructing highly sophisticated, generalized relative concessions and conditions using indefinite terms. This covers structures like *quiconque* (whoever), *quoi que* (whatever - triggering the subjunctive), *où que* (wherever), and *quelque... que* (however/no matter how).

## 31. Present and Past Infinitive Relative Clauses
Forming dense, elliptical clauses using present or past infinitives directly following relative pronouns or prepositional structures without restating the subject (e.g., *Je ne sais quoi faire*, *Après avoir terminé ses études, il est parti*). This optimizes clarity by eliminating redundant subject pronouns.

## 32. Advanced Restrictive, Negative, and Concessive Syntax
Deploying complex, nuanced negative and concessive architectures beyond basic structures. This includes complex items like *ne... guère* (scarcely), *ne... point* (absolute formal negation), *sans pour autant* (without necessarily), and splitting complex conjunctions to elevate argumentation precision.
